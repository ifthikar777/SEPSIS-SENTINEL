Project Information & Study Disclosures

1. Purpose & Limitation Notice

NOTICE: This is a retrospective research prototype and has not been cleared for live clinical diagnostic deployment. All clinical decisions must be verified by the attending physician.

This decision support interface is designed for validation purposes, trained and evaluated on the PhysioNet/CinC 2019 Sepsis Challenge dataset (Combined Sets A & B). Current session: loading…

2. Implemented Baseline Citations

  • Simplified qSOFA: Computes points based on respiratory rate (\(\ge 22\)) and systolic blood pressure (\(\le 100\)). Glasgow Coma Scale (altered mental status) is omitted due to feature missingness. *Reference: Seymour et al. 2016 (JAMA)*.
  • Simplified NEWS2: Computes National Early Warning Score based on heart rate, oxygen saturation, temperature, systolic BP, and respiration rate. Altered consciousness (AVPU) and supplemental oxygen status are omitted. *Reference: Royal College of Physicians 2017 NEWS2 guidelines*.

3. Alarm Fatigue Suppression

ICU alarm fatigue is mitigated by suppressing transient risk spikes (temporary drops or noise). Alarms are only triggered if the Sepsis Risk (≥ 0.3) is sustained for 2 consecutive hours, or is accompanied by severe organ dysfunction indicators (Lactate > 2.0 mmol/L and O₂ Saturation < 90%).

5. Subgroup Power Disclosures

Subgroup metrics are audited for sample size. Any group with fewer than 20 positive sepsis cases is flagged as underpowered to ensure statistical integrity.

6. Alarm Fatigue Suppression Evidence

Retrospective replay of every patient-hour through the suppression logic, comparing raw threshold alarms against suppressed alarms.

7. Label Leakage Audit

SOFA-component features are checked for target leakage by comparing their correlation with the sepsis label at the current hour (t=0) against a 6-hour lookahead (t−6). A large drop implies the feature mostly reflects the label rather than predicting it.
